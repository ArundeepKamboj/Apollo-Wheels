# Master Lease Agreement (MLA)
## Compact reusable template and field guide — Fleet / Equipment Leasing

**Purpose.** A reusable drafting and analysis reference based on the supplied 31-page Caterpillar Financial Services Corporation / CDM Resource Management, LLC Master Lease Agreement dated February 26, 2009, including its Schedule-based structure and Appendix A definitions.

**Business lens.** Fleet and equipment leasing, with practical considerations relevant to a fleet leasing / fleet management business such as Wheels Inc. This is not a Wheels-approved form and does not establish Wheels' actual contractual policies.

**Legal-use warning.** This is a working template, not legal advice or an execution-ready agreement. The source is a US equipment lease containing US-specific legal and accounting concepts. Qualified counsel must adapt it for the correct entities, asset class, governing law, jurisdiction, tax, accounting, and transaction. Do not assume that the source wording or risk allocation is used by Wheels.

---

## 1. How to use this template

The **Master Lease Agreement (MLA)** contains the standing legal framework. Each **Schedule** identifies a particular asset or asset group and its commercial terms. Acceptance certificates, purchase documents, permitted-use documents, and other expressly incorporated documents provide transaction evidence.

Replace every `[BRACKETED FIELD]`. Delete unused alternatives and drafting notes before signature.

| Marker | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `[FIELD]` | Complete with transaction-specific information |
| `[SELECT ONE]` | Select the approved alternative |
| `[IF APPLICABLE]` | Include only where relevant |
| `[LEGAL REVIEW]` | Counsel approval required |
| `[BUSINESS APPROVAL]` | Commercial / operational approval required |

**Recommended workflow:** identify parties and lease model → collect approved commercial and asset data → complete MLA and Schedules → attach supporting documents → reconcile cross-references and calculations → resolve legal / tax / credit / operational approvals → execute and retain the complete document set.

# PART A — MASTER LEASE AGREEMENT

## 2. Title, parties and date

### Template

**MASTER LEASE AGREEMENT**

This Master Lease Agreement (the **“Agreement”**) is dated **[AGREEMENT DATE]** and entered into by:

1. **[LESSOR FULL LEGAL NAME]**, a **[ENTITY TYPE]** organized under the laws of **[JURISDICTION]**, with its principal / notice address at **[ADDRESS]** (**“Lessor”**); and
2. **[LESSEE FULL LEGAL NAME]**, a **[ENTITY TYPE]** organized under the laws of **[JURISDICTION]**, with its principal / notice address at **[ADDRESS]** (**“Lessee”**).

Each is a **“Party”**, together the **“Parties.”**

### Field guide

- **Agreement date:** Establishes the date used for contractual and document-control purposes. Confirm whether effectiveness is on this date or the last signature date.
- **Legal names and entity types:** Must match official entity records. Do not substitute a brand or trading name for the contracting entity.
- **Jurisdiction:** Identifies where the entity was formed; may affect capacity and authority.
- **Addresses:** Identify principal and formal notice addresses. State separately if they differ.
- **Party labels:** Keep “Lessor” and “Lessee” consistent throughout.

**Fleet lens:** The legal owner, funder, contractual lessor, fleet administrator, customer, and vehicle operator may be different entities. Identify each role accurately; do not infer ownership from who manages the fleet.

## 3. Recitals and purpose

### Template

A. Lessor is engaged in **[BUSINESS / OWNERSHIP / FINANCING ROLE]**.

B. Lessee wishes to lease, and Lessor wishes to lease to Lessee, the assets described in Schedules entered into under this Agreement.

C. Each executed Schedule and each document expressly incorporated into it or this Agreement forms part of the relevant Lease Documents.

The Parties agree as follows.

### Why it is needed

Recitals explain the commercial context and intended structure. They should not create unintended warranties. The Schedule mechanism allows repeated asset transactions under a common framework. State clearly which supporting documents are incorporated and which document prevails if terms conflict.

**2.2** Each Schedule will identify the assets, supplier, delivery / acceptance details, location, Term, Rent, options, and special conditions.

### Field guide

The Schedule links the legal agreement to individual assets and transaction economics. A unique Schedule number supports billing, asset administration, and audit. Decide whether defaults, termination, and remedies apply Schedule-by-Schedule or across the whole master relationship. State how assets may be added, substituted, transferred, or removed. An operational fleet-system update does not amend a contract unless the MLA expressly permits it.

## 6. Section 3 — Title and lease characterization

### Template

**3.1 Ownership.** Title to each asset will be held by **[OWNER / LESSOR ENTITY]**, subject to rights expressly granted under the Lease Documents.

**3.2** Lessee's possession or use does not, by itself, transfer ownership.

**3.3** The Parties intend the transaction to be **[TRUE LEASE / FINANCE LEASE / OTHER APPROVED FORM]**, subject to applicable law.

**3.4** The Parties will maintain records sufficient to identify each asset and its ownership status.

### Field guide

Title identifies the legal owner; possession identifies who holds or operates the asset. Lease characterization can affect remedies, accounting, tax, insolvency, and secured-transactions analysis. The contract label alone may not determine legal treatment. Verify title, liens, financing interests, registration, and any ownership / funding affiliates.

## 7. Section 4 — Term, Rent, termination and purchase

### Template

**4.1 Term.** For each Schedule, the Term starts on **[COMMENCEMENT TRIGGER / DATE]** and ends on **[EXPIRY DATE / TERM LENGTH]**, unless ended earlier under the Lease Documents.

**4.2 Rent.** Lessee will pay **[AMOUNT AND CURRENCY]** every **[PAYMENT PERIOD]**, beginning **[FIRST PAYMENT DATE]**, due on **[DUE DATE / RULE]**, by **[PAYMENT METHOD]**. Additional amounts: **[TAXES / FEES / INSURANCE / MAINTENANCE / OTHER]**. Late payment consequences: **[GRACE PERIOD AND APPROVED FORMULA]**.

**4.3 Termination.** A Schedule may terminate only on **[EXPIRY / APPROVED EARLY TERMINATION / CASUALTY / DEFAULT / MUTUAL AGREEMENT]**. Amounts due on termination will be calculated under **[FORMULA / SCHEDULE]**.

**4.4 Renewal.** Lessee **[MAY / MAY NOT]** renew for **[PERIOD]**, by notice no earlier than **[DATE]** and no later than **[DEADLINE]**. Renewal rent: **[AMOUNT / FORMULA / FMV PROCESS]**.

**4.5 Purchase.** Lessee **[MAY / MAY NOT]** purchase the asset for **[FIXED PRICE / FORMULA / FMV]**, by giving notice by **[DEADLINE]** and paying by **[DATE]**, subject to **[CONDITIONS]**.

### Field guide

- **Commencement trigger:** Delivery, acceptance, funding, and in-service dates can differ. State which one starts rent and risk obligations.
- **Rent:** Separate base rent from maintenance, management fees, registration, insurance, taxes, mileage, damage, and pass-through costs. Clarify what is included.
- **Termination:** State each permitted trigger and a reproducible calculation. Avoid discretionary or undefined settlement amounts.
- **Renewal:** Define notice window, duration, and price. This supports replacement-vehicle planning.
- **Purchase:** State whether the option applies per asset or Schedule and address taxes, title transfer, liens, and costs.

### Field guide

Use only options actually offered. Return standards should be measurable and operationally workable. For vehicles, consider mileage, tires, glass, body damage, accident history, maintenance records, keys, accessories, telematics devices, plates, registration documents, and return logistics. Specify how condition disputes and repair estimates are resolved.

## 9. Section 6 — Warranties and disclaimers

### Template

**6.1** To the extent assignable, Lessor will **[ASSIGN / MAKE AVAILABLE]** applicable supplier or manufacturer warranties, subject to their terms.

**6.2** Except for express warranties in the Lease Documents, **[INSERT COUNSEL-APPROVED DISCLAIMER, SUBJECT TO MANDATORY LAW]**.

**6.3** Neither Party may make representations on behalf of the other without written authority.

### Field guide

Clarify whether Lessor promises anything about asset condition, quality, performance, or suitability, and whether supplier warranties pass through. Confirm who handles defects, recalls, warranty claims, downtime, and replacement assets. Do not let a disclaimer contradict separately promised fleet services or service levels.

### Template

**7.1 Maintenance.** **[RESPONSIBLE PARTY]** will maintain each asset in accordance with **[MANUFACTURER / LEGAL / AGREED STANDARD]**, keep service and repair records, and provide them on **[REQUEST / FREQUENCY]**.

**7.2 Alterations.** Material modifications require **[PRIOR WRITTEN CONSENT / EXCEPTIONS]**. The Parties will agree treatment of additions at return or purchase.

**7.3 Inspection.** Lessor may inspect assets and relevant records on **[NOTICE / FREQUENCY / CONDITIONS]**, subject to law and safety requirements.

**7.4 Permitted use.** Assets may be used only for **[PURPOSE / TERRITORY / CONDITIONS]** by **[AUTHORIZED USERS / QUALIFIED DRIVERS]**. Prohibited uses: **[LIST]**.

**7.5 Compliance.** Each Party will comply with laws applicable to its activities and obligations.

### Field guide

Allocate responsibility for scheduled servicing, repairs, parts, accident work, roadside assistance, recalls, and downtime. Identify who approves work and pays vendors. Separate fleet administration from legal responsibility for maintenance. For drivers / users, define licensing, authorization, territory, and prohibited activities. If telematics or location data is collected, address purpose, access, retention, security, privacy, and lawful use.

## 12. Section 9 — Conditions precedent

### Template

Lessor's obligation to deliver, fund, or lease an asset is conditional on satisfaction or written waiver by an authorized person of:

- executed MLA and Schedule;
- supplier / purchase documentation;
- asset identification and delivery evidence;
- signed Acceptance Certificate;
- required insurance evidence and endorsements;

### Field guide

Conditions precedent prevent delivery or funding before required checks are complete. For fleet assets, verify VIN / serial number, supplier invoice, title process, acceptance, insurance effective date, permits, and payment authorization. Identify the reviewer, evidence, waiver authority, and whether waiver must be written.

## 13. Section 10 — Ongoing covenants

### Template

Lessee will provide **[FINANCIAL STATEMENTS / CERTIFICATES]** every **[FREQUENCY]** within **[DAYS]**; provide fleet information including **[MILEAGE / LOCATION / CONDITION / UTILIZATION / MAINTENANCE / INCIDENTS]** through **[CHANNEL]**; notify Lessor of **[CHANGE OF CONTROL / MATERIAL CHANGE]** within **[PERIOD]**; maintain **[FINANCIAL COVENANTS, IF ANY]**; comply with applicable law; and execute further documents reasonably required to implement the Lease Documents.

### Field guide

Covenants are continuing obligations. Financial reporting supports credit monitoring. Fleet reporting supports asset visibility, insurance, maintenance, and remarketing. Change-of-control provisions allow reassessment of risk. Financial ratios need precise definitions and calculation rules. Data and telematics obligations require privacy, security, access, and retention review.

## 14. Section 11 — Assignment, sublease and permitted users

### Template

Distinguish assignment of the contract, transfer of a vehicle, and use by an employee, affiliate, contractor, or customer. Confirm whether Lessor may assign receivables or title to a funding entity. Align the clause with driver authorization and fleet-system records.

## 15. Section 12 — Taxes

### Template

Identify tax type, legally liable party, economic bearer, filing / payment responsibility, invoice treatment, exemptions, refunds, and reassessments. Vehicle taxes vary by jurisdiction. Do not copy US tax concepts into another country without tax advice.

## 16. Section 13 — Casualty, loss and indemnity

### Template

### Field guide

Define notification, repair authorization, replacement vehicle, insurer involvement, deductible, salvage, total-loss valuation, and treatment of outstanding rent. Coordinate the contractual casualty amount with insurance proceeds and loss-payee rights to avoid gaps or double recovery. State who handles claims: customer, fleet manager, owner, insurer, or repairer.

## 29. Economics and risk checklist

For each Schedule, extract and reconcile:

- acquisition / capitalized cost and upfit costs;
- advance payment / deposit;
- periodic base rent and bundled service components;
- taxes, registration, insurance, and pass-through charges;
- Term and commencement trigger;
- mileage / utilization allowance and excess-use charge;
- residual value and FMV assumptions;
- purchase, renewal, early buyout, and termination formulas;
- casualty value, insurance proceeds, deductible, and salvage;
- return-condition and damage charges.

| Risk | Clause | Review question |
|---|---|---|
| Title / liens | Title, purchase documents | Is ownership clear and correctly held? |
| Delivery / acceptance | Schedule, certificate | Who bears risk before / after acceptance? |
| Residual value | Term, options, return | Who bears market-value risk? |
| Maintenance | Maintenance / use | Who authorizes and pays? |
| Casualty | Loss, insurance, indemnity | Does settlement reconcile with insurance? |
| Liability | Insurance / indemnity | Who covers third-party claims? |
| Tax | Tax clause | Who files, pays, and bears cost? |
| Credit | Covenants / default | What monitoring and remedies exist? |
| Data / telematics | Specific data terms | What data is collected and who may use it? |
| Return | Return standard | Are inspection and charges measurable? |

**Analyst test for every clause:** Who is responsible? Which asset / Schedule? What triggers the duty? What is the deadline? What evidence proves compliance? Who pays? What happens on breach? Is it lawful? Can the operating model and systems actually perform it?

## 34. Final principles

1. The MLA is the common framework; the Schedule carries asset-specific details and economics.
2. Ownership, funding, fleet administration, contracting, and vehicle operation may sit with different parties.

4. Operational-system changes do not amend legal documents unless the contract permits them.
5. Insurance must be checked against actual policies and endorsements.
6. US legal concepts must not be assumed to apply in another jurisdiction.
7. Every clause should map to a responsible party, trigger, evidence, cost, and remedy.
8. This is a fleet-oriented working reference, not a Wheels-approved contract.
